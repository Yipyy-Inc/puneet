01What changes, in one page

The current Reputation Booster has a strong feature surface — escalations, protection rules, multi-location, sequencing — but three structural problems stop it being shippable as-is.

  1. The numbers on the page don't reconcile. Overview and Performance describe one location; Locations describes the network; nothing on screen says which. Six KPIs have undefined denominators. Staff review counts exceed total ratings by 31%.
  2. The core promise is a legal problem. "Capture every rating before it goes public… keep negative experiences private" is a description of review gating. That is prohibited by Google's review policies and by the FTC's Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials. The product already contains the correct answer — the open routing note — but it's the non-default, buried in Settings.
  3. The same value is editable in three places. Send delay lives in the trigger card, the send sequence, and the Messages touchpoint. Two independent reminder systems both fire at 48 hours into a "1 per day" cap.

v2 fixes those, then adds the one thing MoeGo does far better: capture at the moment of peak engagement. MoeGo's flagship customer generates 90%+ of their 1,300 reviews from a Review Booster embedded in the grooming report, not from a standalone SMS. Yipyy has no equivalent surface.

The v2 thesis

Ask everyone, ask where they already are, never hide the public option, and put a real service-recovery workflow behind every unhappy answer. Yipyy wins on recovery and multi-location; it needs to catch up on capture.

Finding classes used throughout

D-nn data / logic defect  ·  R-nn feasibility or compliance risk  ·  X-nn configuration or UX contradiction  ·  G-nn capability gap vs MoeGo

Every ID recurs in the phase plan (§16) and the QA matrix (§17), so tickets can be cut directly from this document.

03Feasibility & compliance — what cannot ship as drawn

R-01 · Blocker — review gating

The page header reads: "Capture every rating inside Yipyy before it goes public… keep negative experiences private and resolved." Settings then offers Gate public reviews by rating and an interception threshold defaulting to 4★ & up public.

Selectively showing review links only to happy customers is review gating. Google's review policies prohibit it, and the US FTC's Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, effective October 2024) prohibits suppressing negative reviews. Shipping this as a customer-facing switch exposes both Yipyy and every facility using it.

Required: delete the gating toggle. Open routing becomes the only mode and stops being described as a recommendation. Rename "Interception threshold" → Escalation threshold and change its help text from "treated as public-ready" to "at or below this rating, open a recovery ticket and alert the assignee." Rewrite the page header (§15).

ID Issue Reality Required fix
R-02 Yelp is offered as a routing destination in Channel Manager. Yelp explicitly prohibits soliciting reviews. Routing customers there is a policy violation, not just an ineffective channel. Yelp becomes monitor-only: it can be connected for rating display, but cannot be selected as a solicitation target. Add solicitable: false to the channel record and disable the toggle with an inline explanation.
R-03 Public Reviews cards carry Google / Facebook badges beside Hide and Display buttons. Nothing in Yipyy can hide a review on Google or Facebook. The footnote correctly says the toggle affects the booking page — the badges contradict it. Rename the tab Booking page reviews. Buttons become "Show on booking page" / "Remove from booking page". Badge means "this client also posted to X", shown only when confirmed by sync.
R-04 The tab implies aggregation of live public review content across three platforms. Only Google Business Profile reliably returns review content via API for owned locations. Facebook Page ratings access is heavily restricted; Yelp does not expose review content at all. Two clearly separated data domains: internal survey responses (always ours) and synced public reviews (Google only at launch, per-platform capability flags). Never blend them in one average.
R-05 No quiet hours, no consent surface, no messaging registration state. US SMS requires A2P 10DLC campaign registration and STOP/HELP handling. TCPA restricts contact to 8 a.m.–9 p.m. recipient local time. Canada requires CASL consent and unsubscribe on email. The audit log shows a 4 a.m. send. Add per-location quiet hours (default 09:00–20:00 local), computed from the client's timezone. Sends inside quiet hours defer to the next window rather than drop. Store marketing_consent, consent_source, consent_at, opted_out_at. Block sending if the location's 10DLC campaign is unregistered.
R-06 "Call via IVR" on escalation tickets. IVR is an inbound menu system. This is click-to-call. If the call is recorded, two-party-consent jurisdictions require an announcement. Rename to Call client. Log the call as a ticket event with duration and outcome. If recording is enabled, play a disclosure.
R-07 "Apology credit" issues financial value from a marketing screen. Writes to the billing ledger with no visible cap, approval step, or permission scope. Permission reputation.issue_credit, a per-location cap, an approval step above the cap, and an immutable ledger entry linked to the ticket.
R-08 Multi-language toggle in Messages with no locale source. There is no per-client language field, no translation store, and translated copy changes SMS segment maths (accented characters force UCS-2). Add client.locale; per-locale template variants with an explicit fallback chain; re-run the segment estimator per locale.
R-09 Bulk sends with no pacing. Sudden review velocity spikes trigger platform spam filtering — reviews get collected and then silently filtered out. Per-location daily cap on public-link sends and a jittered send window. Surface projected weekly velocity in Settings.
R-10 Channels store a raw pasted URL (https://g.page/r/…/review). Short links rot when a business profile changes. "Optimize link" has nothing durable to optimise against. Resolve and store place_id; generate the write-review deep link at send time. Keep the raw URL only as a display fallback.

04Configuration conflicts

Several settings can be put into states where the system's behaviour is undefined. Each needs an explicit precedence rule, not just a fix.

ID Conflict Rule to implement
X-01 Send delay is editable in three places: the "Automated Post-Service Trigger" card, Send Sequence step 1, and the Messages touchpoint chip ("SMS · 1 hour"). Send Sequence is the only editor. The trigger card and the Messages chip become read-only mirrors that deep-link to it.
X-02 Global Delivery Channel toggles vs per-step channel dropdowns. Turning SMS off globally while step 1 is SMS has no defined outcome. Global toggle wins. Disabling a channel marks every step using it as invalid, blocks save, and offers one-click reassignment to an enabled channel.
X-03 Two independent reminder systems both fire at 48 h — Send Sequence backup ("2 days, only if no response") and Smart Reminders ("happy-but-silent, 48 hours") — against a "1 per day" cap. One nudge engine, one nudge per request, ever. State decides which: no rating → sequence backup; rated ≥ threshold but no public click → share nudge. Mutually exclusive by construction (§10).
X-04 Client cooldown 30 days vs negative-feedback pause 14 days — precedence undefined. Suppression windows are unioned; the longest applicable window wins. Show the client's next eligible date on their profile.
X-05 Trigger events mix checkout events (Boarding Checkout, Daycare Checkout) with completion events (Grooming Completed, Training Session Completed). A groom inside a boarding stay fires twice. Introduce the visit aggregate: all appointments for one client at one location closing on one business day. One request per visit, regardless of how many services or pets it contains.
X-06 "Invoice Paid" and "Retail Purchase" triggers overlap service triggers. Same visit dedupe. Retail-only visits (no service) get a distinct template or no request at all — configurable, default off.
X-07 Channel Manager shows a chain "Google Business → Facebook Pages" for happy reviewers, with weighting off. Fixed order sends 100% to Google, yet Facebook shows 147 reviews. One primary channel per request. Fixed order = failover only (use next channel if the first is unavailable). Weighted mode = true split. Show the projected split under the list.
X-08 "Interception threshold" is labelled in terms of "public-ready" but, with gating off, only governs escalation. Rename to Escalation threshold; rewrite the help text; move it next to Escalation Routing where it belongs.
X-09 Escalation Routing shows Boarding as "Falls back to default" while All other services shows "1 assigned" — two labels for one mechanism. Uniform display of the resolved chain: service owner → location manager → org default, with the effective assignee named in every row.
X-10 No manual send. There is no way to request a review from an appointment or a client profile. Add "Request a review" to the appointment overflow menu and the client profile, both running the full guardrail ladder with an override reason captured.
X-11 Public Reviews shows "Pending 0" but exposes no approve / reject action. Implement the moderation states in §12 with visible actions and an auto-approve rule.
X-12 The staff table has a "Praise" column implying tags exist, but there is no tag catalog anywhere in Settings. Ship the tag catalog (§08). Praise = count of positive tags attributed to that staff member in range.

05Reading MoeGo — take, beat, avoid

Take — capabilities MoeGo has that Yipyy lacks

ID Capability Why it matters
G-01 Review request embedded in the report card. MoeGo puts the rating widget inside the grooming report the client is already opening. Their published case study attributes 90%+ of 1,300+ Google reviews to this surface. It is the single highest-leverage change in this document.
G-02 Structured tag feedback — "Professional", "Patient & caring", "Clean & tidy", "Responsive" — collected alongside the star rating. Turns free text into queryable data; makes the existing "Praise" column real; gives coaching signal per staff member.
G-03 Reply-with-a-number SMS rating. Client texts "5" — no link, no page load. Removes the biggest drop-off in the funnel. Yipyy is link-only today.
G-04 Manual send from the appointment detail and the client profile. Front desk can ask while the client is still in the lobby.
G-05 Source attribution on every rating (SMS / report card / portal). Without it you cannot tell which surface is worth investing in.
G-06 Per-client review history on the client profile, with total and average. Front desk context; also the natural home for "next eligible date".
G-07 Pet photo attached to showcase reviews on the booking site. Pet photos are the strongest social proof this category has, and Yipyy already stores them.

Beat — where MoeGo is weak

  • Fragile rating detection. MoeGo's inline rating only works if the client replies directly, within 24 hours, with no other message in between. Yipyy should use a 72-hour window, keep an open rating expectation on the conversation that survives interleaved messages, accept digits, words, emoji and star counts, and ask once for confirmation when ambiguous.
  • Report card review booster is SMS-only in MoeGo — explicitly unsupported on email report cards. Yipyy's embed should work on both.
  • MoeGo gates. "Send review link when rating = 5 / 4 and above / 3 and above" is exactly the pattern described in R-01. Do not copy it. Open routing is both safer and, in practice, higher-yield because the eligible pool is larger.
  • No recovery workflow. MoeGo sends a "sorry to hear that" reply and stops. Yipyy's escalation ledger, per-service routing and protection rules are already ahead — finish them with SLAs and a resolution loop.
  • No multi-location view and no protection rules. Keep both; they're the enterprise wedge.
  • Manual showcase selection capped at 20. Yipyy should auto-curate with an override.
Net position

Yipyy is ahead on recovery, governance and multi-location analytics, and behind on capture. v2 keeps the lead and closes the gap: G-01–G-03 are the priority.

07The guardrail ladder

Evaluated in order, short-circuiting on the first failure, and writing a named reason to suppression_log. Order matters: consent before contactability, cheap checks before expensive ones.

Why the ladder runs twice

With a one-hour delay between checkout and send, a refund can be opened, an incident logged, or a STOP received inside the window. Re-running at send time is cheap and prevents the single most damaging failure mode this feature has: asking for a review from someone whose pet was just injured.

08Capture surfaces

Six entry points, one survey_response record. Source is always stored, so surface performance becomes measurable — which is how you decide where to invest next.

SMS inline rating — parser requirements G-03

  • Open a rating_expectation on the conversation with a 72-hour TTL. It survives interleaved messages — this is where MoeGo breaks.
  • Accept: digits 1–5, words in the client's locale (five, cinq), star characters, and rating-bearing emoji. Normalise before matching.
  • If the reply contains a number and other text ("5 thanks so much"), take the rating and store the remainder as the comment.
  • If ambiguous, send exactly one clarifying message. No answer within TTL → the expectation closes silently; the request follows the normal expiry path.
  • Immediately reply with the routing message (§09) containing the public link — that reply is not a "nudge" and doesn't consume the nudge budget.

Tag catalog G-02 X-12

Tags are per-service-type, configurable per location, with a polarity so they can drive analytics:

Service Positive tags (shown at ≥4★) Improvement tags (shown at ≤3★)
Grooming Great cut · Gentle handling · Clean finish · On time Cut not as asked · Rushed · Pet seemed stressed · Late
Boarding Pet came home happy · Great updates · Clean facility · Attentive staff Few updates · Pet seemed unsettled · Cleanliness · Check-out delay
Daycare Tired & happy · Good socialisation · Easy drop-off Little info at pick-up · Pet seemed anxious · Drop-off queue
Training Clear progress · Patient trainer · Good homework Session felt rushed · Not enough one-on-one · Unclear next steps

The improvement tags are the important half: they convert "2★, session felt rushed" from prose a manager must read into a countable signal. The existing Training escalation on the Escalations tab is exactly this case.

09Routing & amplification

Channel rules

  • One primary destination per request. The current "Google Business → Facebook Pages" chain implies both, which splits attention and converts worse.
  • Fixed order = failover. Use the highest-priority enabled channel; fall through only if it's unavailable or unlinked.
  • Weighted mode = true split. Deterministic assignment by hashing request_id, so re-renders are stable. Show the projected 30-day split under the channel list.
  • Store place_id, not URLs R-10. "Optimize link" resolves a pasted Maps URL to a place ID and previews the resulting write-review deep link.
  • Yelp is monitor-only R-02, with an inline note explaining why the toggle is disabled.

10Timing engine — one nudge, ever

This resolves X-01, X-03, X-04 and D-11 in one mechanism.

Rules

  • nudges_remaining starts at 1 and is decremented on any nudge send. Nothing else can send it.
  • Every scheduled job carries a TTL. If now > scheduled_at + ttl, the job is dropped and logged as EXPIRED_JOB. This is the direct fix for the 49-day reminder.
  • Suppression windows are unioned: next_eligible_at = max(cooldown_end, negative_pause_end, manual_hold_end).
  • Quiet hours defer within the day; if deferral would push past the step TTL, the step is skipped, not delayed indefinitely.

11Escalation & service recovery

This is Yipyy's strongest differentiator against MoeGo, which does nothing beyond an automated apology reply. Finishing it means adding time pressure and a closing loop.

Routing resolution

Display the resolved chain in every row so the effective assignee is never implicit X-09:

service_assignees(service_id) → e.g. Grooming: David Wilson ↳ if empty: location_manager(location) → e.g. Plateau: Manager One ↳ if empty: org_default_assignee → Admin User Every assignee in the resolved set is alerted and receives a follow-up task.

Incident linkage

The 1★ Daycare escalation on the current build reads "Nala had a cut on her leg when I picked her up and staff didn't mention it." The Protection Rules already contain "Block when booking has a critical incident" — but nothing created the incident. Add: when a response contains injury/illness language, or an improvement tag with severity: high is selected, prompt the assignee to create or link an incident record. That closes the loop that currently exists only in one direction.

12Data model

Notes for implementation

  • Config is versioned. campaign_version is immutable; a request stores the version it ran under. Otherwise editing a template retroactively changes what you believe you sent.
  • Scope is denormalised onto review_request so every analytics query can filter by location without joining through visit → appointment.
  • Attribution is single-valued. attributed_staff_id is set from visit.primary_staff_id, or from the survey's "who looked after Nala?" answer when the visit had more than one staff member. Any "mentions" metric is derived separately and labelled as such.
  • Suppressions are first-class rows, not log lines. They power a Suppressions view and answer "why did only 312 of 480 checkouts get asked?" — a question the current build cannot answer at all.

18Open questions for product

  1. Default escalation threshold. 3★ or 4★? At 3★ the current data gives roughly 6% of responses escalating (~15 tickets per 247). At 4★ it's ~14% (~35 tickets). Pick based on how much recovery capacity a facility actually has.
  2. Showcase minimum. Only 5★ on the booking page, or 4★ and up? An all-5★ wall reads as manufactured; 4★ and up is more credible and roughly doubles the pool.
  3. Retail-only visits. Ask for a review after a retail purchase with no service? Default off is proposed; confirm.
  4. Staff visibility of negative feedback. Should a groomer see their own 2★ reviews directly, or only through their manager? This is an HR decision that determines the notification scope enum.
  5. Google review sync. Requires each location to complete Business Profile OAuth. Who drives that onboarding — support, or a self-serve prompt in Settings?
  6. Re-invite after resolution. If a 1★ complaint is resolved to the client's satisfaction, do we ask again after the cooldown? It's the single highest-upside move in the flow and also the highest-risk if the resolution was superficial. Proposal: manual, assignee-initiated, one attempt.
  7. Locations scope for non-network accounts. Single-location facilities shouldn't see the Locations tab at all — confirm it hides rather than showing a leaderboard of one.
